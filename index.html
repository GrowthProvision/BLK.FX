Hedging, in plain English

Fix the price now. Reduce surprises later.

Exchange rates move all the time. If you need to pay or receive money in another currency later, a worse rate could affect your budget. Hedging is simply a plan to reduce those surprises.

Full protection

Lock in all of a future amount.

Partial protection

Lock in some, keep some flexible.

Spread it out

Combine forwards and spot conversions to smooth the average rate over time.

Understand your options

Beyond the rate

The rate is only part of it.

Most people compare FX providers on the rate. The real cost sits in the parts nobody quotes: when you convert, how the payment travels, who you call when it doesn't arrive, and what happens to the numbers you've already promised.

01

Hidden margins

Banks rarely charge a visible fee. The cost is built into the rate, often 1–3.5%, and it is taken on every conversion.

02

Timing

Converting on the invoice date is a decision, just not a deliberate one. A market view and a plan can be worth more than a better rate.

03

Payment routing

Intermediary banks can deduct charges on the way, so the beneficiary receives less. We route locally where possible.

04

Delays and missing payments

A stuck payment costs relationships, not just money. You have a named person who investigates and chases, 24/7.

05

Budget risk

A 5% currency move can wipe out a margin you have already reported. Forward contracts fix the rate so the budget holds.

06

Service when it matters

Weekends, completions, deadlines. Real people, no bots, day or night.
